# The hillside's look, from real photos

Every photo here was opened and looked at. They live in `../fleeced-journal/img/refs/`, private, with `SOURCES.md` beside them; none goes in this repository. Colors are the mean sRGB of a measured patch of the named photo, not guesses (`[r, g, b]`). The game is cartoony: copy the shapes and colors, leave out the detail.

## The one idea

The real scene is two worlds with a dyke between them: bright grazed green inside the wall, tawny rough hill outside (`fold-meggethead`, `fold-cramalt`, `tree-henderland`). A dark wall line across a smooth green slope with one tree on the skyline is the whole picture (`tree-henderland`, `turf-kennetsideheads`).

## Turf

- The game takes hill pasture, not lowland lawn: duller and strawier, [130,147,93] (`fold-meggethead`), [95,124,54] (`turf-eastertoun`), with a brightness spread of 17-28 of 255 across a patch (R1.1100).
- Lawn in the photos: short, even, saturated green, finely speckled, not flat: [103,138,48] sunlit (`turf-byrewalls`), [94,126,37], [126,155,71] (`turf-manor`), [84,115,36] duller (`turf-kennetsideheads`).
- Soft blotches of darker and paler green a few meters across, and low hummocks (`turf-manor`, `turf-eastertoun`).
- Sparse rush clumps standing proud of the sward, dark at the foot and straw at the tips, [131,127,58], loose in wet lines (`turf-scabcleuch`, `tree-henderland` foreground). These are the one tall thing in the grass.
- Trampled ground is a real motif: bare earth and tan grass where sheep funnel through a gap (`wall-gateway`) and under a lone tree where they shelter (`tree-lone-tall`).
- Longer, darker grass and tussocks along the wall's foot, where sheep can't reach (`wall-galloway-a`, `wall-stane-a`).

## Dry stone walls (Galloway dykes)

- The structure is the look, and `wall-fetlar` shows it best. Bottom third: flat stones in two rough courses, a double wall. Middle: a single row of big stones set on end, leaning, with daylight in the chinks. Top: rounded cope stones, lumpy, the skyline of the wall.
- Stones are big and few, not brickwork: the top stones reach a third of a meter or more (`wall-stane-b`, `wall-knockman`).
- Copes lean the same way along a stretch, then change (`wall-troquhain`, `fold-patieshill`).
- Mean stone: [107,109,101] (`wall-stane-a`), [111,108,79] warm (`wall-lunky`), [109,115,115] (`wall-fetlar`). Runs of wall go warmer or bluer together. White lichen flecks on upper faces, gold in places, moss in the joints near the top and on the shaded side (`wall-gateway`, `wall-knockman`). Joints are near black.
- A wall end at a gap is a vertical cheek of squarer stones stacked long and short, capped by one big stone, sometimes a post beside it (`wall-gateway`, `fold-flotterstone`).
- A few loose stones lie in the grass at the foot (`wall-lunky`, `tree-sycamore-old`).
- Far off it is a thin dark line that wanders with the ground (`wall-knockman`, `turf-byrewalls`).

## Lone trees

- Sycamore: `tree-sycamore-old` is the one to copy. Short massive trunk, thick limbs forking low, a broad dome of rounded lobes with the lower lobes drooping well out to the sides. Dark: lit [73,88,50], shade [51,65,27], much darker than the turf, so it reads from anywhere. Bare branches in `tree-sycamore-foulden` show the vase of limbs.
- Rowan: small, leaning, rooted on a rocky knoll, a wind-flattened umbrella of a few distinct clumps with sky between them (`tree-rowan`).
- Scots pine: a stout leaning trunk under a heaped crown of many irregular round clumps, streaming one way, every tree leaning the same way (`pine-broomy`, `pine-culloden`). Flat pads on thin limbs read as bonsai in the game, so the pads of `pine-affric` are not copied.
- All of them lean with the prevailing wind. Round each: worn ground, a boulder or old dyke stones at the foot (`tree-lone-tall`, `tree-sycamore-old`).

## Scots pines, added after the rest

- `pine-affric`: salmon-orange trunk above, gray and plated below, on a knoll; flat layered pads on S-curved limbs. `pine-culloden`: the same pads, more open, on a sheltered slope. `pine-bog` shows old stumps and is not used.

## Cairns and rock

- A cairn is a heap of rounded boulders, biggest at the bottom, stepping in to a small stack of flat stones on top (`cairn-toul`). Paler and pinker than dyke stone: [128,119,114].
- Outcrops are low whalebacks of rock barely proud of the turf, cracked into slabs, with grit and grass in the cracks: [116,115,112] (`cairn-william`).

## Folds

- Sheep folds are drystone rings ten to twenty meters across, or squared pens, sitting low on the hill, with one narrow opening between two cheeks (`fold-patieshill`, `fold-meggethead`, `fold-stell-a`, `fold-flotterstone`). The map's Fold is a walled corner, so it borrows the opening, the cheeks and a timber gate (`wall-gateway`).
- `fold-dip` is a concrete dip, no use as a Fold: it is here for its turf.

## Light and sky

- A high-latitude sun, low and soft, long shadows (`fold-patieshill`, `wall-galloway-a`).
- Clean sky: zenith [65,123,177], mid [82,140,196], and it pales fast to [197,218,238] at the horizon (`turf-scabcleuch`, `fold-stell-a`). Big fluffy clouds [217,223,230], often a bank along the horizon (`tree-syart`, `tree-henderland`).
- Hills go blue with distance: [145,161,168], then [107,151,184] (`turf-byrewalls`, `wall-stane-b`); on a damp day they melt to [208,215,216] (`turf-manor`).
- Past the wall the far hills are rounded, overlapping and paler with every ridge (`wall-stane-b`, `tree-northmid`). Near ones: olive [119,119,82], heather [103,81,60] (`tree-northmid`, `pine-broomy`). Far: [96,140,172] (`wall-stane-b`).
- Open moor is bent grass [125,125,67] with heather worked into it, never a solid patch (`pine-broomy`).

## Left out on purpose

Fences, pylons, wind turbines, stiles, conifer plantations, snow.
